Encumbrance Certificate (EC)

Apply for and read an Encumbrance Certificate - the registered transaction history of a property for a chosen period.

Encumbrance, Title and Property VerificationEncumbrance CertificateAvailable - Phase 1

What is this service?

An official extract of registered transactions affecting a property for a chosen period.

An Encumbrance Certificate lists the transactions registered against a property for a chosen period at the Sub-Registrar Office. Buyers, lenders and heirs use it to check for registered mortgages, sales and other dealings. It reflects only registered transactions - it is one input to title verification, not the whole of it.

Why and when you need it

Why
It shows registered sales, mortgages, gifts and other dealings, and is routinely required by buyers and banks.
Who needs it
Buyers, sellers, lenders, heirs and anyone verifying a property's registered history.
When
Before purchase, before lending, after a dispute is suspected, or when a bank asks for it.

Required documents

  • Property schedule details - survey number, site or property number, boundaries
  • Village or municipal details of the property
  • Period for which the EC is required
  • Identity proof of the applicant

Identity and supporting documents

  • PAN card of each applicant or party
  • Aadhaar card or another government photo identity and address proof
  • Passport-size photographs
  • For companies and associations: incorporation documents, board resolution and authorisation letter

Step-by-step procedure

  1. Confirm the exact property schedule from the title deed
  2. Decide the period the EC should cover
  3. Apply through the department's online services or at the SRO
  4. Pay the applicable fee
  5. Download or collect the certificate and read every entry carefully

Legal requirements
  • Registration Act, 1908 - EC is drawn from the registration records
Common rejection reasons
  • Property schedule does not match registration records
  • Wrong SRO jurisdiction selected
  • Incomplete applicant details
Important cautions
An EC shows only registered transactions - unregistered agreements, possession claims and litigation may not appear
A nil-encumbrance entry for a period does not prove clear title by itself
Match the EC entries with the title documents - every entry should be explainable

FAQs

Does a clean EC prove clear title?
No. An EC records registered transactions only. Title verification also checks the parent documents, Khatha, tax, revenue records, approvals and litigation.
How far back should the EC go?
Long enough to cover the full title chain you are verifying. For most purchases, take advice on the period - commonly several decades or since the root of title.
What about transactions before computerised records?
Older records may need a manual search at the SRO. The department's certified copy page covers records before 01-04-2004.
