Will Drafting and Registration Assistance

Legally structured Wills for property succession - drafting, execution guidance and optional registration.

Property Inheritance and Family TransfersWill drafting assistanceAvailable - Phase 1

What is this service?

A testamentary document disposing of property after death.

A clear Will prevents the most painful family property disputes. It must name the executor, identify properties precisely and dispose of them unambiguously. Registration of a Will is optional but adds evidentiary strength. Drafting is done with an independent qualified lawyer.

Why and when you need it

Why
Without a Will, succession law divides the estate - often not as the owner intended.
Who needs it
Every property owner, especially with multiple heirs or specific intentions.
When
Now - a Will can be updated any number of times.

Required documents

  • List of properties with title particulars
  • Intended distribution and executor details
  • Identity proof of the testator

Identity and supporting documents

  • PAN card of each applicant or party
  • Aadhaar card or another government photo identity and address proof
  • Passport-size photographs
  • For companies and associations: incorporation documents, board resolution and authorisation letter

Step-by-step procedure

  1. List the estate and the intended distribution
  2. Draft the Will with an independent lawyer
  3. Execute with two witnesses
  4. Register optionally; store safely and tell the executor where it is

Legal requirements

  • Indian Succession Act, 1925 as applicable
  • Registration Act, 1908 - Wills may be registered

Common rejection reasons

  • Ambiguous property identification
  • Missing witnesses or execution defects
  • Later conflicting documents

Important cautions

Review the Will after major life events
Do not treat nomination as a substitute for a Will

FAQs

Must a Will be registered?
No - registration is optional but strengthens the record.
Can a Will be changed?
Yes, by a later Will or codicil executed the same way.
Who should draft it?
An independent qualified lawyer - the cost of a defective Will falls on the family later.
